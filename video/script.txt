01|feed|The F D A publishes a food enforcement report every time it classifies a recall. Two thousand five hundred and forty seven of them since the start of last year, from eight hundred and fifteen different firms. Six hundred and fourteen are still open today. A food pantry gets these as email, addressed to nobody, written for a regulator.
02|gap|Look at what a notice actually gives you to check a shelf against. A third publish a lot code. A third publish a barcode. And twenty seven percent publish nothing checkable at all. So if you go looking for a lot code when the notice lands, a quarter of those walks end at a case whose code cannot be compared to anything.
03|intake|Best By moves the reading to the only moment it is easy. A volunteer books in a donation, the case is already in their hands, and photographing the stamped panel costs four seconds. This is the F D A's own photograph of the bottom of a recalled can. The model reads the lot code, S 8 8 N D 1 M, and the best by date. It says not present for the barcode, because there is no barcode on the bottom of a can.
04|match|Six weeks later the notice arrives. This is the live feed, and this is the entire string the firm published to identify the affected units: best by dates, one seventeen twenty twenty eight, lots, S 8 8 N D 1 M. Free text, typed by a regulatory affairs person. The comparison is now exact, and the engine names what decided it: the lot is among the recalled codes.
05|split|Here is the whole pass against every open recall. Twenty three intake lots, two thousand two hundred and thirty nine units on the shelf, three hundred and nineteen already handed out. And notice what it does with a match. It splits it. Cases still on the shelf get pulled. Units that already went home with a household can only be told. Those are different jobs, and the second one is the only half that reaches a person who might otherwise eat it.
06|gate|Telling a family their food is recalled is not reversible, so it is the one thing the agent cannot do alone. It drafts the notice, files the disposal record, and stops. In a run with nobody watching, that case simply waits, and the pass moves on to the next recall instead of hanging.
07|sends|When the coordinator approves, the notices go out through S E S and every household line carries the message id that proves it. Households with a child under five are listed first, because a botulism notice names under fives as the population at risk. To be exact about what is real here: the recalls, the match, the sends and these message ids are real. The pantry roster is representative, and those addresses are on a domain this project controls. No family was contacted.
08|veto|The guardrail is a hook that cancels the send unless the ledger holds a match, a distribution record and a drafted notice. Here it is failing on purpose. Make it return before it can refuse anything, and two tests go red, including the one whose failure message says the quiet part: with the hook removed, this case would have mailed households about soup that is not in the recalled batch. Put it back, and a hundred and twenty tests pass.
09|unattended|None of this waits for anyone. EventBridge runs the Lambda at seven in the morning in Dayton, an hour before the first distribution shift, because a recall check that lands at midnight is a recall check nobody reads. Six hundred and fourteen open recalls, two hundred and two pairs judged, in about eight seconds. And every case writes the record a county health inspector actually asks for, including the section nobody else keeps: the lots that were checked and found not affected.
10|close|Best By is for the coordinator at a pantry serving three hundred households a month, who got the same email everybody got and has no way to act on it. The food most likely to reach someone who cannot absorb the consequences is the food that already left the building. Built with the Strands Agents S D K, on A W S.
